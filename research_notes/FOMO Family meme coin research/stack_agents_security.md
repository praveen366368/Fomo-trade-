## 1. Latency: where the time goes in Solana meme-coin sniping/trading, and whether C++/OCaml beat Rust/Go/TypeScript

### Takeaway
Almost all of the latency you can win or lose comes from three places. (1) **How early you see the event**: the feed type, from fastest to slowest, is shreds (Jito ShredStream or similar), then Yellowstone/Geyser gRPC, then WebSocket JSON-RPC, then REST polling. (2) **Physical distance** to the RPC, the leader and the block engine: tens to 100+ ms. (3) **How the transaction lands**: staked connections/SWQoS, Jito bundles or tips, Helius Sender-type multi-path services, priority fees. Code execution (parse, decide, build, sign) takes microseconds to low milliseconds in any compiled language. Slots are ~400 ms and shrinking toward ~200 ms in late 2026, so that execution time is a small share. C++ or OCaml gives **no meaningful edge over Rust** here, and Rust has the Solana ecosystem. TypeScript is acceptable outside the top "sub-slot" tier, especially when its I/O and decoding run in native Rust addons.

### Cited Findings
**Data feeds (event visibility)**
- One vendor ranks feeds, at equal region, hardware and network path, as UDP shreds first, then Geyser gRPC, then WebSocket JSON-RPC notifications (2025–2026) — [RPC Fast: ShredStream vs Geyser vs standard RPC](https://rpcfast.com/blog/shredstream-vs-geyser-vs-standard-rpc); [RPC Fast: Solana data streaming guide](https://rpcfast.com/blog/solana-data-streaming-guide)
- Yellowstone gRPC has lower latency than WebSocket because it sends Protobuf over HTTP/2 with server-side filters — [Chainstack: WebSocket vs Yellowstone gRPC Geyser](https://chainstack.com/?p=29354); [Helius gRPC page](https://helius.dev/solana-grpc)
- Shred feeds reportedly arrive ~50–100 ms before Yellowstone. Shreds lack metadata, logs and final execution status, while replay-based gRPC carries inner instructions, balance changes and logs but arrives later. Several teams use a hybrid: shreds as the early trigger, gRPC for consistent state (vendor claims, 2025–2026) — [RPC Fast streaming for AI trading agents](https://rpcfast.com/blog/solana-data-streaming-for-ai-trading-agents); [eRPC: Solana data streams (Dec 3, 2025)](https://erpc.global/en/news/2025/12/03/erpc-solana-app-speed-data-streams-ws-grpc-udp-shreds/)
- ShredStream cuts reaction latency but raises build cost: you own the decoding, deduplication and reconciliation against confirmed state — [RPC Fast](https://rpcfast.com/blog/shredstream-vs-geyser-vs-standard-rpc); [Corvus Labs latency stack](https://docs.corvus-labs.io/platform/latency-stack)
- Shyft (a Jito competitor) benchmark in New York: its RabbitStream arrived first in 97.19% and 96.52% of transactions across two runs, and Jito ShredStream p99 held near ~80 ms. These are competitor numbers, not independent — [Shyft: RabbitStream vs Jito ShredStream](https://shyft.to/blog/rabbitstream-vs-jito-shredstream)
- Helius is deprecating its LaserStream "Preprocessed" gRPC feed in favour of a standalone WebSocket stream of decoded shreds (2026). The "WebSocket = slow" rule therefore applies to standard JSON-RPC PubSub, not to shred-decoded WS products — search summary of [Helius gRPC/LaserStream pages](https://www.helius.dev/solana-geyser-streaming)

**Geography and RPC location**
- A distant validator-to-agent link, e.g. a Frankfurt validator and a US-hosted agent, adds ~80–120 ms round-trip "before your code runs a single line" (vendor, 2025–2026) — [RPC Fast: Solana AI agents for HFT](https://rpcfast.com/blog/solana-ai-agents-for-hft)
- Infrastructure vendor Dysnix: if strategy logic is sound and you still lose races, "the bottleneck is almost certainly your data feed or submission path—not the strategy" — [Dysnix: Solana arbitrage bot setup](https://dysnix.com/blog/solana-arbitrage-bot-setup)
- Jito block engine regional endpoints include New York, Amsterdam, Frankfurt and Tokyo, and regional endpoints are recommended for lower latency — [Chainstack: Jito explained](https://chainstack.com/jito-explained-bundles-tips-mev-solana/); [QuickNode Jito bundles guide](https://www.quicknode.com/guides/solana-development/transactions/jito-bundles)

**Transaction landing**
- Jito bundles are 1–5 transactions executed atomically with tip-based ordering, sent with `sendBundle`. Tips are separate from priority fees and go to one of 8 tip accounts (`getTipAccounts`). The block engine forwards bundles to leaders running Jito-Solana, about 95% of stake "at time of writing", and reportedly takes a 5% fee on tips (third-party docs, 2025–2026) — [Alchemy: Jito bundles](https://www.alchemy.com/docs/chains/solana/solana-jito-bundles-and-transactions); [Chainstack: Jito explained](https://chainstack.com/jito-explained-bundles-tips-mev-solana/); [RPC Fast: Jito 2026](https://rpcfast.com/blog/jito-explained-bundles-tips-mev-solana)
- Helius Sender sends each transaction to several paths at once (Helius, Jito, Harmonic, Rakurai). It claims typical land time of ≤1.5 slots (vendor claim) — [Helius Sender docs](https://www.helius.dev/docs/sending-transactions/sender); [Helius Sender FAQ](https://www.helius.dev/docs/faqs/sender)
- Helius minimums conflict across its own pages: the FAQ gives a 0.0002 SOL minimum tip for Jito auction participation, while Sender Max requires 0.001 SOL and a priority fee of at least 5,000 lamports (10,000 recommended). Check current docs — [Helius Sender FAQ](https://www.helius.dev/docs/faqs/sender); [Helius Sender Max](https://www.helius.dev/docs/sending-transactions/sender-max)
- Counterpoint from Helius itself: auctions add delay that "could actually make transaction landing times worse than sending a well-optimized transaction through staked connections" — [Helius blog: zero-slot execution with Sender and LaserStream](https://www.helius.dev/blog/zero-slot)

**Protocol changes in late 2026 (they shrink the windows)**
- Alpenglow activated on Solana mainnet on Oct 21, 2026 (epoch 1064, ~13:36–14:00 UTC; sources differ on the minute). The target finality is ~150 ms, down from ~12.8 s under TowerBFT, and independent measurements were still pending at launch — [The Block (Oct 21, 2026)](https://theblock.co/news/ecosystems/2026-10-21-anzas-alpenglow-goes-live-on-solana-cutting-finality-to-150-milliseconds-421508); [Solana.com Alpenglow page](https://solana.com/upgrades/alpenglow); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/solanas-alpenglow-launches-mainnet-99-faster-finality)
- Solana cut slot time for the first time since genesis in Aug 2026 and completed stage 4 of a slot-reduction plan in Oct 2026. The plan aims to halve slot times to ~200 ms. The exact current slot time was not found — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/solanas-alpenglow-launches-mainnet-99-faster-finality)

**Language vs network**
- In Helius's JS gRPC (LaserStream) SDK, network I/O and protobuf deserialization run in Rust, outside the JS event loop. Helius claims a ~40x throughput increase for its JS client from this — [Helius blog: LaserStream SDKs](https://www.helius.dev/blog/laserstream-sdks)
- RPC Fast says pure TypeScript struggles with raw shred decoding at Jito volumes, and that "for anything competing in the sub-slot latency tier, Rust is not optional". Dysnix says TypeScript is fine for prototypes and for strategies "not racing the top of the searcher pack" — [RPC Fast: Jito ShredStream Rust/TS/Go](https://rpcfast.com/blog/integration-guide-jito-shreadstream-rust-typescript-go); [Dysnix trading bot guide](https://dysnix.com/blog/solana-trading-bot-guide)
- An anecdotal sniping write-up reports Rust ~3x faster than equivalent JS, without published methodology — [dev.to: "I sniped a Solana token in 400ms"](https://dev.to/apollo_ag/i-sniped-a-solana-token-in-400ms-heres-the-full-tech-stack-25b6)
- A 2023 benchmark compared JS, Python and Rust for MEV bots. Its numbers were not retrieved — [Solid Quant on Medium](https://medium.com/@solidquant/how-fast-is-your-mev-bot-comparing-javascript-python-rust-72376a820291)
- An Ed25519 signature costs ~87,548 CPU cycles on 2011-era Intel hardware, roughly 30 µs at 3 GHz, and less on modern CPUs *(training knowledge, not re-verified this session)* — [ed25519.cr.yp.to](https://ed25519.cr.yp.to/)

### Inferences
- **Rough latency budget for one snipe (inferred from the sources above; order of magnitude only):**
  | Stage | Typical cost | Lever |
  |---|---|---|
  | Seeing the event (shreds vs gRPC vs WS vs REST poll) | 50–100+ ms difference between shreds and gRPC; REST polling adds your whole poll interval | Feed type |
  | Network distance (agent ↔ RPC/leader/block engine) | ~80–120 ms if on the wrong continent | Co-locate in NY, Amsterdam or Frankfurt; never run the hot path from a home connection in India |
  | Decision + tx build + Ed25519 sign | microseconds to ~1 ms in Rust/C++/Go; a few ms p99 in TS/Go if GC pauses or JSON parsing are careless | Language and code quality (small) |
  | Landing (leader inclusion) | ~1–2 slots (≈200–800 ms, depending on the current slot time) | Staked/SWQoS path, Sender, Jito tip, priority fee |
  | An LLM call (if wrongly put in the path) | ~0.07–0.5 s (Jev, vendor claim) to ~10 s (frontier LLM, vendor table) = 1 to 25+ slots | Keep it out of the path (see Section 4) |
- Implication: rewriting a well-written Rust executor in C++ might save tens of microseconds. Moving the box from Mumbai to Frankfurt or New York, switching from WebSocket to gRPC or shreds, and using a staked landing path each save **tens to hundreds of milliseconds**. Spend engineering effort in that order.
- Alpenglow and shorter slots (Oct 2026) shrink every reaction window. Feed choice and co-location matter more than before, and any per-trade model call matters even less.
- REST polling should not be used anywhere in the trade path. Keep it for slow background tasks such as metadata lookups. No source quantified this; it follows directly from the push-vs-poll design.

### Gaps
- I found no independent, third-party benchmark comparing shreds, gRPC and WebSocket head to head. All numbers are vendor or competitor claims.
- The exact mainnet slot time after the Oct 2026 reductions, and how Alpenglow changes Jito auction timing, were not found.
- Whether Jito ShredStream is still offered in its 2024–2025 form in Nov 2026 is ambiguous: Jito's docs index lists it, while one Chainstack guide describes it in the past tense.
- Round-trip time from Indian ISPs or data centres to Frankfurt or New York was not sourced.

## 2. Solana client libraries by language (C++, OCaml, Rust, TypeScript, Python, Go) and whether C++/OCaml signing is realistic

### Takeaway
Rust (Anza/Solana Foundation crates, Yellowstone and Jito clients) and TypeScript (`@solana/kit`) are the first-class ecosystems. Python is well served by `solana-py` and the Rust-backed `solders`. C++ has only small community SDKs (`solcpp`, originally for Mango Markets), and I found **no OCaml Solana SDK at all**. Signing and sending a Solana transaction from C++ or OCaml is technically realistic, since Ed25519 is available through libsodium, mirage-crypto-ec or HACL*. The real cost is hand-porting and maintaining the message format, ALTs/v0 messages, PDAs, and every DEX/launchpad instruction layout (Pump.fun, PumpSwap, Raydium, Meteora, Jupiter), plus Jito tip instructions. That makes C++/OCaml a correctness liability with no measurable speed gain (see Section 1).

### Cited Findings
- **C++ – solcpp**: "C++ SDK for Solana and Mango Markets" at github.com/mschneider/solcpp. It ships examples including `examples/sendTransaction.cpp`, needs a C++17 compiler, and installs the core `libsol` as a static lib. Community forks exist (Draviener/solcpp-SDK, bdhobare/solcpp). Maintenance status and stars could not be retrieved — [awesome.ecosyste.ms: mschneider/solcpp](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fmschneider%2Fsolcpp); [Draviener/solcpp-SDK](https://github.com/Draviener/solcpp-SDK); [bdhobare/solcpp](https://www.github.com/bdhobare/solcpp)
- **OCaml – no Solana SDK found.** Ed25519 is available in `mirage-crypto-ec` (RFC 8032 signatures as r‖s; v1.1.0 API `priv_of_octets`; older 0.9.x used Cstruct-based `priv_of_cstruct`), `hacl-star` (formally verified HACL*) and `tezos-crypto`. An OCaml Discuss thread also suggests the ahrefs `ocaml-sodium` libsodium binding. No OCaml base58 or Solana serialization library surfaced — [ocaml.org mirage-crypto-ec Ed25519](https://ocaml.org/p/mirage-crypto-ec/1.1.0/doc/Mirage_crypto_ec/Ed25519/index.html); [ocaml.org hacl-star Ed25519](https://ocaml.org/p/hacl-star/0.6.1/doc/Hacl_star/Hacl/Ed25519/index.html); [ocaml.org tezos-crypto Ed25519](https://ocaml.org/p/tezos-crypto/17.3/doc/Tezos_crypto/Signature/Ed25519/index.html); [discuss.ocaml.org: best library for Ed25519](https://discuss.ocaml.org/t/best-library-to-generate-ed25519-signatures/16716)
- **Rust**: The Solana Foundation's `solana-keychain` crate provides one signing interface over many backends (local, KMS-style, etc.), and Chainstack documents its use. Many Rust signer crates exist (e.g. `signer-svm`, built on ed25519-dalek) — [docs.rs solana-keychain README](https://docs.rs/crate/solana-keychain/latest/source/README.md); [Chainstack: solana-keychain signing](https://docs.chainstack.com/docs/solana-keychain-signing.md); [docs.rs signer-svm](https://docs.rs/signer-svm)
- **Rust** is the reference language for Yellowstone gRPC and Jito ShredStream integrations, with TS and Go guides as secondary options — [RPC Fast: Jito ShredStream Rust/TS/Go](https://rpcfast.com/blog/integration-guide-jito-shreadstream-rust-typescript-go)
- **TypeScript**: `@solana/web3.js` 1.x is a maintenance branch (security fixes only, no new features), and `@solana/kit` is the successor recommended by Solana docs. Kit is smaller and class-free — [web3.js 1.x maintenance README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/solana-foundation/solana-web3.js@maintenance/v1.x/README.md); [Chainstack: common web3.js errors](https://docs.chainstack.com/docs/common-errors-with-solana-web3js)
- **TypeScript (single source, unverified)**: a "web3.js v3" announced Oct 6, 2026 reportedly rebuilds nine legacy classes on top of Kit, with v1 moving to maintenance-only — [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/solana-web3js-v3-javascript-sdk-launch/)
- **Python**: `solana-py` 0.36.12 was released May 14, 2026. It is "the base Python library for building transactions and calling the Solana JSON RPC API" and relies on `solders`, a Rust-written Python toolkit that includes a litesvm testing module. Solana docs now credit kevinheavey for both, and also list Anchor-py — [PyPI mirror: solana 0.36.12](https://simple-repository.app.cern.ch/project/solana); [Solana docs: Python SDKs](https://solana.com/docs/clients/community/python)
- **Go**: Go Solana packages expose `Transaction.Sign` (fork of the widely used gagliardetto/solana-go) — [pkg.go.dev nik-bm/solana](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/nik-bm/solana)
- **Agent SDKs**: Solana Agent Kit (SendAI) and GOAT are TypeScript-first SDKs, and ElizaOS is a runtime that mounts them as plugins. GOAT's main branch was reportedly untouched since Aug 19, 2025, with the last core npm release in May 2025. Rust agent developers are pointed to Rig — [Alchemy: Solana Agent Kit vs GOAT vs ElizaOS](https://www.alchemy.com/overviews/solana-agent-kit-vs-goat-vs-elizaos); [Alchemy: how to build Solana AI agents in 2026](https://www.alchemy.com/blog/how-to-build-solana-ai-agents-in-2026)
- A Solana transaction is a compact array of 64-byte Ed25519 signatures plus a message (header, account keys, recent blockhash, compiled instructions; v0 adds address lookup tables), with a 1232-byte packet limit. libsodium's `crypto_sign_detached` produces a standard 64-byte Ed25519 signature *(training knowledge, not re-verified this session)* — [Solana docs: transactions](https://solana.com/docs/core/transactions); [libsodium: public-key signatures](https://doc.libsodium.org/public-key_cryptography/public-key_signatures)

### Inferences
- **Is C++/OCaml signing realistic?** Yes. Ed25519 signing (libsodium in C++; mirage-crypto-ec, HACL* or an ocaml-sodium binding in OCaml) and base58 are both simple. The hard and risky part is everything around them: v0 messages and ALTs, compute-budget instructions, ATA and PDA derivation, the exact account lists for Pump.fun, PumpSwap, Raydium and Meteora instructions, and Jito tip transfers. Rust and TS get these from maintained SDKs and Anchor IDLs. A C++/OCaml port must be redone on every program upgrade, and a single wrong account order or a missing slippage field loses money.
- **Recommended language/runtime per component** (my synthesis):
  | Component | Recommended | Why / alternative |
  |---|---|---|
  | Feed ingest (Yellowstone gRPC, shred decode), event normaliser | **Rust** | Reference clients are Rust; pure TS struggles with shred decoding |
  | Hot-path filters (rug/honeypot checks on mint/freeze authority, LP, holder concentration), sizing | **Rust** (same process or shared memory) | Deterministic, no network calls |
  | Tx builder, signer, sender (Sender/Jito/staked RPC fan-out) | **Rust**, with the signer in a separate locked-down process | `solana-keychain`-style abstraction lets you swap a local key for a policy-enforced remote signer |
  | Risk/policy engine (caps, allowlists, kill switch) | **Rust**, or **OCaml** if the team really wants it (Jane Street pattern, Section 3) | Must be synchronous with the executor; keep it a small pure-function module or an IPC service |
  | Orchestration, LLM/Jev adapters, Telegram/UI, agent framework | **TypeScript** (`@solana/kit`, Solana Agent Kit) or Python | Seconds-scale; ecosystem matters more than speed |
  | Research, backtesting, analytics, tax ledger | **Python** (`solders`, `solana-py`, pandas) | Fast iteration |
  | C++ | Only if the team already has strong C++ and wants to own shred decoding | No speed win over Rust at Solana's ms-scale budgets; weakest Solana ecosystem |
- If the team insists on "C++ for speed", the most defensible compromise is Rust for anything that touches Solana bytes, with C++ limited to an optional numeric or signal library called through FFI.

### Gaps
- GitHub stars and last-commit dates for solcpp, solana-agent-kit, TradingAgents, FinRobot, ElizaOS, Kit, solders and anchor were **not retrievable** in this session (GitHub API blocked), so maintenance claims are unverified.
- The status of the Rust `anchor-client` crate in 2026 was not found.
- The current Solana docs and libsodium pages were not fetched to confirm transaction-format details.

## 3. How Jane Street uses OCaml in trading, and whether the pattern fits (OCaml for risk/strategy rules, C++ for the hot path)

### Takeaway
Jane Street chose OCaml mainly for **correctness, readability and fast development**, through its strong static types. Its speed comes from a large investment: compiler extensions (OxCaml) and FPGA/ASIC hardware designed in OCaml (Hardcaml). A small meme-coin team cannot copy that toolchain. The part of the pattern that fits is **"strong types for the rules that guard money"**: risk limits, order-state machines and policy checks. A C++ hot path is the norm in exchange-colocated HFT with microsecond budgets, but Solana's budget is milliseconds and network-bound, so Rust is a better hot-path choice than C++ here.

### Cited Findings
- Jane Street picked OCaml because its "rich type system improves the quality of their code and catches bugs early". The firm has 500+ OCaml programmers and 30M+ lines of OCaml (OCaml.org, undated current page). A 2008 paper reported ~20 OCaml programmers and hundreds of thousands of lines at the time — [OCaml.org success story: Large Scale Trading System](https://ocaml.org/success-stories/large-scale-trading-system); [Minsky & Weeks, "Caml Trading" (JFP 2008)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/02F18023B4C43BF6E53512AA7062A9A5)
- A 2003 rewrite of core trading systems in Java was dropped partly because the code was "too difficult to read and reason about". The 2005 OCaml rewrite produced a first prototype in three months and was trading three months after that — [Caml Trading paper (Cambridge Core)](https://core-prod.cambridgecore.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/02F18023B4C43BF6E53512AA7062A9A5/S095679680800676Xa.pdf/caml-trading-experiences-with-functional-programming-on-wall-street.pdf); [ACM Queue: "OCaml for the Masses" (2011)](https://queue.acm.org:443/detail.cfm?id=2038036)
- Jane Street says it writes its **lowest-latency software systems in OCaml** and has built major OCaml extensions in recent years — [Jane Street: Performance engineering](https://www.janestreet.com/performance-engineering/)
- "Introducing OxCaml" blog post (Jun 14, 2025, Leo White): OxCaml is Jane Street's set of OCaml extensions (oxcaml.org). A Blind forum comment claims these enable stack-allocated locals and unboxed types (anecdotal) — [Jane Street technology page](https://janestreet.com/technology/); [Jane Street blog](https://blog.janestreet.com/page/2); [Blind (anecdotal)](https://www.teamblind.com/post/distributed-systems-concepts-in-hft-auynud2f)
- Hardcaml is an OCaml-embedded hardware toolchain that Jane Street's ultra-low-latency team uses to design, test and deploy ASIC/FPGA hardware. Its FPGA engineer postings list $250k–$300k base salary (2025–2026) — [Jane Street: Advent of Hardcaml 2024](https://blog.janestreet.com/advent-of-hardcaml-2024/); [Jane Street FPGA Engineer posting](https://www.janestreet.com/join-jane-street/position/4274809002)
- The "Safe at Any Speed" tech talk covers building a performant, safe, maintainable packet processor in OCaml — [Jane Street tech talk](https://janestreet.com/tech-talks/safe-at-any-speed)
- Jane Street's "Why OCaml" post explains the choice — [blog.janestreet.com/why-ocaml](https://blog.janestreet.com/why-ocaml/)

### Inferences
- The Jane Street lesson is not "OCaml is faster than C++". It is that expressive types make a trading codebase safer to change quickly, and that ultra-low latency took custom compilers and hardware. For FOMO Family, that argues for:
  - **OCaml (optional) or Rust for the risk/policy kernel**: variant types for order/position states, exhaustive pattern matching so an unhandled state fails to compile, pure functions with property-based tests. Keep it tiny and run it in-process or over local IPC so it adds microseconds, not milliseconds.
  - **Not OCaml for the Solana hot path**, because no OCaml Solana SDK exists (Section 2) and every program layout would need hand-porting.
  - **Not C++ for the hot path either.** The C++/FPGA tier wins microseconds at co-located exchanges, while Solana trades are won or lost on feeds, distance and landing paths measured in milliseconds (Section 1). Rust gives C++-class speed plus the Solana ecosystem plus memory safety, which is the closest analogue to Jane Street's "safety at speed" goal.
- Running two compiled languages (OCaml + C++) alongside a TS/Python agent layer means three or four toolchains for a small team. That operational cost is likely to exceed any latency benefit.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch the talk transcripts or the OxCaml docs to quote specific latency numbers or techniques.
- I found no Jane Street commentary on crypto DEX/on-chain trading specifically.

## 4. Multi-agent LLM trading frameworks and research (TradingAgents, FinRobot, ElizaOS, Virtuals, Solana Agent Kit, GOAT, 2025–2026 systems), and why LLMs stay out of the sub-second hot path

### Takeaway
Published multi-agent frameworks split roles into analysts (fundamental/sentiment/news/technical), debating researchers (bull/bear), a trader, a risk team and a final approver. They report backtest gains on equities, not live meme-coin results. The only notable live test, Nof1's Alpha Arena Season 1 (Oct–Nov 2025), showed most frontier LLMs **losing money**, with trading costs from overtrading dominating PnL. LLMs are 1 to 25+ slots slow, non-deterministic, and exposed to prompt injection via attacker-controlled token names, metadata and social posts. They belong in a **warm/cold advisory lane** that adjusts bounded parameters (watchlists, scores, regime flags). They should not be in the sub-second execution path and should never hold signing authority.

### Cited Findings
- **TradingAgents** (arXiv:2412.20138; v1 Dec 28, 2024; v7 Jun 3, 2025; Xiao, Sun, Luo, Wang): LLM agents in specialised roles (fundamental, sentiment, news and technical analysts; Bull and Bear researchers who debate; traders with varied risk profiles; a risk-management team). The authors report improved cumulative return, Sharpe ratio and max drawdown versus baselines **on historical data**. It was an oral at the AAAI 2025 Multi-Agent AI in the Real World workshop and an ICML 2025 workshop poster. The code is at TauricResearch/TradingAgents with a "research purposes, not financial advice" disclaimer, and v0.2.0 (Feb 2026) added multi-provider LLM support — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.20138); [ICML 2025 listing](https://icml.cc/virtual/2025/49302); [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.20138)
- **Trading-R1** (arXiv:2509.11420, Sep 2025, overlapping authors): applies LLM reasoning plus reinforcement learning to trading — [arXiv via TradingAgents search results](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.20138v6)
- **FinRobot** (arXiv:2405.14767, May 2024, AI4Finance Foundation): open-source multi-agent platform in four layers (Financial AI Agents using Financial Chain-of-Thought; financial LLM algorithms; LLMOps/DataOps; multi-source LLM integration). A follow-up (arXiv:2411.08804, Nov 2024, ICAIF 2024 workshop) uses three agents (Data-CoT, Concept-CoT, Thesis-CoT) for equity research — [FinRobot arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14767v1); [FinRobot equity research arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.08804v1)
- **Solana Agent Kit (SendAI)** is an SDK for your own agent loop, with 60+ actions including perps trading, Pump.fun launches and Jito bundle audit trails (older ElizaOS brief). **GOAT (Crossmint)** is a multi-chain toolkit with stalled maintenance (main branch untouched since Aug 19, 2025). **ElizaOS** is a heavier full runtime (personas, memory, social clients) that mounts Solana Agent Kit or GOAT as plugins. All are TS-first (2026 guides) — [Alchemy comparison](https://www.alchemy.com/overviews/solana-agent-kit-vs-goat-vs-elizaos); [ElizaOS: Solana AgentKit brief](https://elizaos.github.io/knowledge/docs/partners/solana-agentkit/brief.md); [Coingape: best Web3 AI agent frameworks 2026](https://coingape.com/best-web3-ai-agent-frameworks/)

### Inferences
**Why LLMs stay out of the hot path (synthesis):** (1) Latency: even the fastest "decision model" (Jev, vendor-claimed 70–500 ms) uses one or more ~200–400 ms slots, and general LLMs take seconds. (2) Non-determinism: the same input can yield different trades, which makes risk unbounded and post-mortems hard. (3) Attack surface: meme-coin names, descriptions, Telegram/X posts and website text are attacker-written inputs, so an LLM reading them can be steered. (4) Evidence: live LLM traders in 2025 mostly lost money, mainly through overtrading and fees. (5) Cost per decision at high event rates.

## 6. Wallet and key security: hot/burner patterns, key storage, caps, allowlists, simulation, malicious bots and fake GitHub snipers, embedded-wallet key export

### Takeaway
The dominant real-world loss vectors for retail bot traders in 2023–2026 have been **(a) trading-bot platforms holding user keys** (Banana Gun, Maestro, Unibot, Bonkbot/Solareum, Bloom) and **(b) fake "sniper bot" repos, packages and apps that steal keys from `.env` files or the clipboard** (SlowMist Jul 2025, JFrog "FakeFix" 2026, Kaspersky Feb 2025, fake "Padre" app). Exotic on-chain attacks are a smaller factor. The design that follows from this: a small funded **hot/burner trading wallet** refilled from a policy-guarded vault, keys **never in plaintext env vars**, a signer in a separate process (or a TEE/KMS-style service) that enforces **daily caps and a program allowlist**, simulation, and strict supply-chain hygiene, including for AI coding agents.

### Inferences
**Concrete wallet architecture for the bot:**
1. **Cold treasury**: hardware wallet (Ledger etc.), manual only, never on the bot server.
2. **Warm vault**: a policy-guarded server wallet (Turnkey or Privy server wallet, or your own HSM/KMS-backed signer if it supports Ed25519). Its only allowed action is topping up the hot wallet to a fixed ceiling, at most N times per day.
3. **Hot/burner trading wallet(s)**: hold at most ~1 day's risk budget. Rotate addresses regularly (also limits copy-traders and front-runners profiling you). Use separate burners per strategy so one compromise doesn't drain all.
- **Key storage ranking** (worst → best): plaintext `.env`/env var (the exact target of the 2025–2026 stealers) → encrypted keyfile unlocked at start-up by a passphrase entered by hand → OS keyring or secrets manager injecting into a single signer process → remote signer with an enforced policy (TEE/KMS/HSM). The trading process never sees the raw key and only sends unsigned transactions to the signer.
- **Signer-side rules (not agent-side)** that an LLM or a compromised agent cannot bypass:
  - per-tx and per-day SOL caps
  - max open positions
  - **program allowlist**: System, Compute Budget, SPL Token/Token-2022, ATA, the specific launchpad/DEX programs, and Jito tip accounts
  - block `SetAuthority`/`Approve` to unknown delegates
  - destination allowlist for any SOL/USDC transfer out
  - a kill switch that freezes signing
- **Simulation:** run `simulateTransaction` on the exact message for non-urgent paths (exits, transfers, new program versions). For the snipe path, where a round trip costs time, pre-simulate transaction *templates* when a program or config changes and rely on on-chain slippage/min-out guards. Simulation does not protect against post-simulation state changes, so min-out and slippage limits remain mandatory.
- **Supply-chain hygiene:** never run third-party "sniper bots" from GitHub or Telegram. Pin and lock dependencies and audit any package with "solana", "jito" or "pump" in its name. Run AI coding agents in a sandbox **without access to the key file or the production `.env`**, given the SolanaCry case.
- **FOMO app export:** if the user exports a key from FOMO into the bot, treat that address as permanently "hot". Better: create a fresh bot wallet and fund it from the FOMO wallet, rather than importing the FOMO key into a server.
- Telegram is a delivery channel for drainers and fake support. The bot's Telegram control interface should be **read-mostly**: status, pause and kill. It should never accept key material or allow withdrawals to new addresses.

## 7. India legal and tax points (VDA tax 2026, TDS 194S, loss rules, FIU-IND, offshore DEX/app use, 2025–2026 changes) plus wash-trading/market-manipulation notes

### Takeaway
As of Nov 2026, India still taxes VDA transfer gains at a **flat 30% + surcharge + 4% cess**. Only the cost of acquisition is deductible, and **losses cannot be set off against any income (including other VDA gains) or carried forward**. A **1% TDS** applies on transfers: formerly s.194S, re-numbered under the new **Income-tax Act, 2025 (in force 1 Apr 2026)**, reportedly as **s.393(1), Table Sl. No. 8(vi)**. Budget 2026 (Feb 2026) kept the rates but added crypto-entity reporting from 1 Apr 2026, with penalties. Crypto is neither banned nor specifically regulated. VDA service providers serving Indians must register with **FIU-IND under PMLA**: 54 had registered by Mar 9, 2026, and FIU-IND blocked rounds of non-compliant offshore platforms in Dec 2023, Oct 2025 and Sep 2026. Each Solana swap (SOL→meme coin, meme coin→SOL) is a separate taxable VDA transfer. A high-frequency bot with no loss set-off can therefore **owe tax even while losing money overall**. Bots must also avoid self-trading and coordinated pumping: the U.S. DOJ prosecuted crypto wash-trading market makers in 2024–2025.

### Cited Findings
**Tax rates and loss rules**
- Budget 2026 (Feb 2026) kept VDA transfer income at a flat 30% plus surcharge and cess. Sections 115BBH and 194S continued, with no cut to the rate or TDS. **TDS**
- The old s.194S maps to **s.393(1) [Table Sl. No. 8(vi)]** of the 2025 Act from Apr 1, 2026 (TDSMAN, May 2026; CryptoSlate). A TaxGuru comparison writes "393(8)(vi)" instead, so the sub-clause citation is inconsistent across sources. Per CBDT FAQs as reported, credits or payments on or before Mar 31, 2026 stay under the 1961 Act, and later ones fall under the 2025 Act — [TDSMAN blog (May 2026)](https://blog.tdsman.com/2026/05/tds-on-virtual-digital-assets-section-3931-194s/); [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/crypto-laws/india-income-tax-act-vda-tax-tds-regime/); [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/?p=1081077)
- The rate stays 1%. One source says thresholds were removed in the 2025 Act, while others cite ₹50,000 (specified persons) / ₹10,000 (others). **Verify against the official text** — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/?p=1081077); [Analytics Insight](https://www.analyticsinsight.net/news/india-keeps-30-crypto-tax-as-new-vda-reporting-rules-take-effect); [Income Tax Dept TDS-on-VDA page](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/tds-on-payment-for-the-transfer-of-virtual-digital-assets-vdas-)

On **DEXs** such as Jupiter there is no identifiable counterparty, making TDS "practically impossible to implement", with no 26QE equivalent for DEX trades. **Every DEX swap is still a VDA transfer** under s.2(47A) and taxable at 30% (vendor view: KoinX) — [KoinX: TDS on foreign exchanges and DEXs](https://www.koinx.com/in/tax-guides/tds-on-crypto-trades-on-foreign-exchanges-and-dexs/); [ClearTax: TDS under 194S](https://cleartax.in/s/tds-under-section-194s-of-income-tax-act)

### Inferences
- **Tax arithmetic that should shape the bot design.** Suppose winners gross ₹10 lakh of gains and losers lose ₹9 lakh. Net trading profit is ₹1 lakh, but tax is 30% of ₹10 lakh = ₹3 lakh + 4% cess (≈₹3.12 lakh), before surcharge. **The account ends ~₹2.1 lakh down after tax.** Meme-coin sniping is high-churn with many small losers, so the strategy must clear a much higher bar in India than its pre-tax PnL suggests. Fees, priority fees and Jito tips are also not deductible under s.115BBH, which allows only cost of acquisition *(based on the 115BBH regime as widely described; confirm with a CA)*.
- **Every swap counts.** SOL→token disposes of SOL, and token→SOL disposes of the token, so each round trip creates two taxable transfers, each needing an INR valuation at the time of the trade. Agent #20 (Section 4) should log, per swap: timestamp, tx signature, both legs, INR rate source, cost basis (FIFO or another consistent method) and TDS status. Hundreds of trades a day make this unmanageable by hand.
- **TDS on DEX swaps** is legally unresolved, with no counterparty to deduct. Keep records and get a CA's view on whether self-deduction or reporting is expected. Fiat on/off-ramps should go through FIU-registered Indian exchanges, where TDS is handled.
- **Offshore DEX and app use.** No law found makes it illegal for an Indian resident to use a non-custodial wallet or a DEX. However: (a) the tax obligations above apply in full; (b) custodial or "exchange-like" offshore apps serving Indians without FIU-IND registration risk being blocked under PMLA s.13, as in the 2023/2025/2026 rounds, which could freeze access to funds; (c) whether the FOMO app (or its operator) is FIU-registered was **not** confirmed. Check the FIU-IND registered list before keeping material balances there.
- **Wash trading and manipulation:** the bot must never trade between wallets the team controls on the same token. That includes several "FOMO Family" members' wallets, which would look like coordinated self-dealing. It must also not coordinate group buys with public promotion (pump-and-dump), or generate fake volume. The DOJ cases show these are prosecuted as fraud even when the token is tiny. India has no specific crypto market-abuse statute, but general fraud/cheating law and PMLA could apply *(inference; not researched)*. Build a risk-gate rule that blocks a trade if the counterparty or LP wallet belongs to the team.
