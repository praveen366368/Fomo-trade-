Fees and odds sink the $1-per-coin plan

Six research tracks, one live sample of 255 new Solana coins, and our own 20-agent bot paper-trading on live data all point the same way. Buying $1 of many new meme coins and selling at a target loses money after costs. The engine is worth keeping as a lab and a safety screener.

Plain answer: do not fund $1-per-coin auto-trading. Keep the bot in paper mode, prove an edge on recorded data first, and if it ever goes live use $2–$5 stakes, a separate burner wallet, and budget for India's 30% tax with no loss offset.

About 1 new coin in 250 hits 10x, and insiders get there first

Solana makes new meme coins at a huge rate. Bitquery counted 11.5 million new Solana tokens from Nov 2025 to Oct 2026, 78% from pump.fun, about 34,000 a day. 79% of pump.fun coins never traded again after launch day, and only 1 in 58 "graduated" from pump.fun's starting price curve to a real pool. On 9 Nov 2026 just 312 of about 24.4 million pump.fun coins were worth more than $100,000.

"Thousands of percent in minutes" does happen, but a 10x is about a 1-in-250 event and the window to sell lasts minutes. Coins that graduate do it in a median 1.66 minutes; right after, 38% fall 80% within 10 minutes. Coin creators and their bundled wallets get supply cheap or free and took at least $579M. Of 2.9 million Solana meme buyers, 74% held less SOL two weeks later, and spreading money across more coins did not help. Putting $1 into many coins does not fix a game where the average coin loses. It only makes the loss smoother.

FOMO is a big, real app, but it rules out this plan

FOMO is run by FOMO Labs Inc. (Delaware), founded by ex-dYdX people. It has raised about $94M: a $17M Series A led by Benchmark (Nov 2025) and a $75M Series B led by Index Ventures (Jun 2026) at a reported $550M valuation. It claims about 2.5M users. Wallets are self-custody Privy wallets and the key can be exported. No hack is confirmed; a $6M drain claim in 2026 was denied and never proven. Watch for the fake lookalike site fomoo.family.

Three rules block $1 auto-trading in the app

  • No bots. The Terms (§16) ban bots, scripts, scrapers and sharing session tokens. There is no public API, and FOMO can close accounts it suspects.
  • Market orders only. Spot has no take-profit, stop-loss or limit orders. "Copy trading" is only an alert you act on by hand.
  • Minimums. Fee 0.5% per side (0.45% with a referral), with "a minimum of up to 0.95 USDC on smaller Solana trades". The app's code sets a $2 minimum buy and sell.

FOMO's own users show what its crowd achieves. Bitquery traced 829,539 Solana wallets that traded through FOMO from Jan to Oct 2026: about 10% were profitable after fees and the median wallet lost $25. Fees were only about a tenth of the losses; the rest came from the coins. India is not on FOMO's banned-country list, and VPN use is banned.

What one $1 round trip really costs

Trading on-chain from your own wallet avoids FOMO's minimums, but $1 is tiny next to fixed costs. pump.fun charges 1.25% per side. Each open coin locks about 0.002 SOL (about $0.15) of account rent, refunded when the account is closed. A typical 0.001 SOL "landing tip" alone is about 7% of $1 per side.

FOMO app: $2 minimum order; a $0.95 minimum fee would take 95% of $1. Even at the lean 3.3%, the 255-coin sample lost money with every exit rule. Practical minimum stake: about $2 for a simple round trip, about $5 for a sell ladder.

The lean figure is a floor, not a forecast. During research a real Jupiter quote on a freshly graduated coin returned $0.933 for $0.994 put in, a 6% gap on one side alone. One small upside: at $1, sandwich bots barely bother you. Still keep slippage tight; those bots took $29.7M from wide-slippage meme trades in 11 months.

Five trades prove almost nothing about skill: with a 6.3% chance of touching 2x, five buys see no 2x about 72% of the time. What the run does show is how costs behave. The 3% landing drift each way is a deliberate worst-case guess and explains about 5 of the 19 points lost. Without it, all five trades still lose about 14% each, because prices drifted down and real sell quotes came in below the screen price.

The filter audit follows rejected coins afterwards. GTA, rejected because its creator had rugged coins before, fell 99.4% during the run. All six coins cut by the deep checks fell afterwards and none reached 2x. Of 101 rejected coins we followed, 84 went dead. The "older than 24 hours" rule skipped one coin that later did 2.6x.

Both flaws the report found are fixed and tested (commit on branch claude/tender-cerf-sdzlws):

  • Screen price vs real price. Exits were decided on Dexscreener's price (up to 30 s old) but sold at Jupiter's real quote. Across the five sales the real quote ranged from 37.5% below to 7% above the screen. Now every position is marked at a real sell quote, peaks track sellable value, and no exit fires until a fresh quote confirms it.
  • Leaky hard reject. OGTA was hard-rejected (round trip lost 12.4%), fell out of the full 400-coin watch list, came back as a boosted lead and was bought at score 100. Hard rejects now go on a permanent ban list.
  • Time stop now matches the simulated rule (leave at 5 min if below 1.3x). Tests: 126 engine + 18 risk, all passing.

Run 2 (15 minutes with the fixes): 1,872 leads, 18 deep checks, 4 buys, 1 win (AI, +$0.07), total −$0.22, about −5% per trade. Marks now match what a sale returns: AI showed +10.8% sellable and returned $1.07, where in run 1 a coin showing +2.1% on screen returned $0.89. Four trades prove nothing about an edge.

The "$160 to $100k" FOMO traders win by doing the opposite

Nobody in the evidence matches "$1–$100 to lakhs in 3–4 months" with checked on-chain proof. The closest is Spyzer, who FOMO says turned $160 into nearly $100k in a month, with no wallet or trade record published. 0xGolden's story shares word-for-word quotes with another trader's profile, so at least one is credited to the wrong person. Media stories like $160 to $6.14M on Chill Guy are single lucky bets, often with insider claims. By Jan 2025 only 0.4% of 13.4 million pump.fun wallets had made $10,000 or more.

What the winners say they do

  • Avoid new pairs; buy coins at $400K–$3M market cap (Washed).
  • Take the starting money out after a 2x and let the rest ride (Logjam).
  • Hold swing trades 3–7 days; win only about 38% but much bigger (Spyzer).
  • "$1,000 spread across 20 positions… cannot move your life" (llonchain).
  • Counter-example: 4pfCoyote lost $250K, partly chasing "19 people you follow bought" alerts.

Their pattern is few coins, later entries, bigger bets, held for hours or days and sold in steps: the opposite of many $1 bets sold within minutes. Copying them works poorly too. Smart-money wallets averaged about 14% while copiers kept about 3% after costs; following the earliest group-buy signal lost in 32 of 32 setups; copies placed two minutes late lost 41% per coin.

How to study them anyway: by hand, in the app. Use the Positions leaderboard (filter by amount invested) and the trades leaderboard's smallest size group ($100–$1k). Favour 30-day consistency, many trades, a 40–60% win rate and trade sizes like yours. Pulling that data with a script breaks the Terms. Paste chosen public wallets into config/wallets.csv; the bot gives their buys only a +15 score bonus and still makes every coin pass every filter.

Data stack (all keyless today)

Dexscreener (prices, 0.21 s average), GeckoTerminal (new pools; rate-limited 20% of calls), RugCheck (contract and holder report, 2.5 s), Jupiter lite-api (quotes, Shield NOT_SELLABLE flag, organic-volume stats), Solana public RPC. Fine for coins at least 3 minutes old, which the engine targets on purpose; far too slow for the first two minutes. Before live: move to api.jup.ag with a free key and the Swap API v2. Buy faster data (PumpPortal websocket free, Helius or a gRPC stream about $49/month) only after a replay shows earlier entries help.

Filters that matter most

  • Yes-or-no traps, one miss costs 100%: active mint or freeze authority, Token-2022 permanent delegate, transfer hook, transfer fee or non-transferable flag, failed sell quote. All rejected.
  • The first five minutes of trading are the best-studied warning signs (F1 about 0.72–0.79 on the trained venue, about 0.38 elsewhere).
  • Holder counts hide a lot: after merging linked wallets, the true top-10 share of risky coins was 24% higher.
  • Raw volume flatters fakes: at least 17% of trades are wash trades.

Exit rules: never sell 100% at a fixed target

The owner's "sell at X%" rule is the weakest exit in every test the team has. The live sample and the simulation agree: tight fixed targets lose, higher targets lose less, and holding without a plan is worst.

Trailing stop: arm at 1.5x, sell 30% below peak, leave at 5 min if no move defaultnot testednot tested+23 to +25¢

The trailing stop was the only rule with a positive average in the model, and only thanks to rare coins that run past 50x. Cap prices at 50x and it falls to about −6¢; remove the best 0.1% of trades and it drops to −7¢. A public backtest of 4,293 pump.fun callouts turned negative when the best 24 were removed. So the trailing stop is the right default, not a money machine. Test 30%, 40% and 50% trails on recorded data. The owner's rule remains available as exit_mode = fixed.

Stack: keep C++ and OCaml, let Jev score text only

On Solana, speed is won in three places: how early you see events, how close your server is, and how your trade lands. Each costs tens to hundreds of milliseconds, while the code runs in microseconds in any compiled language. A server on the wrong continent adds about 80–120 ms. Solana's Alpenglow upgrade (live 21 Oct 2026, about 150 ms finality) shrinks every window further. C++ gives no real speed edge over Rust here, and its Solana toolkit is thin; OCaml has no Solana library at all. Jane Street's lesson is that OCaml's strict types catch bugs in the rules that guard money, which is exactly how the RiskGovernor uses it.

For live trading: let Jupiter's Swap API v2 build each transaction and sign it in a small separate signer process. Do not hand-write pump.fun instruction layouts in C++. Run the trading loop from New York, Amsterdam or Frankfurt, not from India.

Jev

"JEV" is almost certainly Jev, TypeSafe AI's "System One" decision model, announced 15 Sep 2026 and still in waitlist early access. It answers typed questions about text or JSON with a value and a probability in a claimed 70–500 ms, and knows nothing about markets beyond what you send it. Minor versions have broken compatibility. It fits agent 15: scoring tweets, Telegram posts and coin descriptions for hype or scam patterns. Pin the version, never give it keys, never let it size or place trades. In Nof1's Alpha Arena, most top AI models lost money trading real crypto. "JVI" matched nothing and is probably a typo.

Security: a separate burner wallet with hard limits

Bot traders mostly lose money through stolen keys, not bad trades. Banana Gun refunded about $3M drained through its bot; a fake pump.fun bot on GitHub stole keys from .env files; in 2026, 25 fake Solana npm and PyPI packages hunted for keypairs.

  • A separate bot wallet holding only one day's risk ($50–$250), refilled from a vault. Not the FOMO wallet.
  • A separate signer process holds the key and enforces a daily cap and a short program allowlist (Jupiter, pump.fun, token programs).
  • Keys never sit in config or environment files. A Telegram bot may show status, pause and kill, nothing more.
  • Never trade between team members' wallets on the same coin. That is wash trading: FOMO bans it and US prosecutors punish it (Gotbit forfeited $23M).

India's tax turns a break-even bot into a losing one

India taxes crypto gains at a flat 30% plus 4% cess. Only purchase cost is deductible, and losses cannot offset any gain or be carried forward. Budget 2026 left this unchanged. A 1% TDS still applies (old 194S, now section 393(1) of the Income-tax Act 2025 from 1 Apr 2026); on a DEX, who deducts it is unsettled. Every swap is a taxable transfer valued in rupees at that moment.

Keep a per-swap rupee ledger from day one (agent 20 can do it), move money through an FIU-registered Indian exchange, and see a chartered accountant before the first live trade. India blocked 15 more unregistered offshore platforms in Sep 2026; FOMO's registration status is unknown.

The deeper problem is that the plan's two goals fight each other. The checks that keep a bot safe (wait three minutes, demand real liquidity, real buyers and a sellable quote) put it after the move. Two honest options remain: copy the FOMO winners' style, with the engine as a screener and safety checker for a few larger, later bets; or keep the engine as a lab and accept that it may prove there is no edge to find.
